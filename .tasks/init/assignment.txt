Ash’s Pokémon Card Collection Assignment
Ash Ketchum is probably the world’s biggest Pokémon card fan. He has been collecting
Pokémon cards since day one. Ash has faced many Pokémon card trials and defeated
countless rival players on his path. Now, he faces his greatest challenge ever: finding anything
in his card collection.
Your task is to build Ash a home for his collection: one place to start from, where he can find any
card in seconds and keep his favourites close at hand. The collection can be found in the
provided ash_collection.json. You are also free to use the source API directly at Pokémon
TCG API.
Assignment Overview
Your goal is to build a small web app for Ash’s card collection. We are interested in how the app
is put together and how it feels to use. You have creative freedom in how to approach this, but
here are some feature suggestions to get started:
Find a card fast: He remembers part of a name. How quickly can he get to the card?
Browse: Which sets, types and rarities does he own, and what is in each?
Look at one card: Everything about a single card on one page he can send to a friend.
Keep favourites at hand: The cards he cares about most should be there when he opens
the app.
Come back later: What he searched or saved should still make sense after a reload, or
on his phone.
Requirements
1. Tech:
React and TypeScript are required
Any other libraries and tools are up to you
2. Deliverables:
A homepage that works as Ash’s starting point into the collection.
Navigation between the pages, such as a menu or tabs.
A list or grid of the collection with search and filtering, kept in the URL so a filtered
view can be shared as a link.
A detail view for a single card, for example split into tabs for attacks, weaknesses
and legalities.
One small personal touch that survives a reload, such as pinning favourite cards to
the homepage.
It should work on a phone as well as a laptop, be usable from the keyboard, and
handle loading and empty states sensibly.
3. AI Usage:
You are allowed to use AI coding assistants such as Claude Code and Codex to
speed up development time. GitHub Copilot and similar assistants are fine too. The
design must be your own. We will judge code quality so use AI responsibly.
4. Submission:
Share an URL to the app or use a video demo.
Share the code using a public Git repository that includes a README with:
Setup instructions.
A short explanation of your approach and any trade-offs made.
Documentation of unimplemented features.
5. Time Expectation:
Spend no more than half a day on this project. If you are short on time for all the
deliverables or have more ideas but not enough time, feel free to document them
instead of implementing everything.
